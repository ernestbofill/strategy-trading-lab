- 2026-11-24 · A Manual strategy follows the user's trades: only signals the user did are bought or sold in the rules' replay. A skipped or missed signal (or one still waiting, or a buy the budget held back) buys nothing, but still stands, so its window gives no second signal while open; once the window closes and the conditions apply again, the same step signals again. "The rules alone" is gone (figure, chart line, the "Rules: · You:" line), and Manual signals get no signal trades (the old rows are deleted by a migration, Ernest's yes). A missed signal takes only the trade the user made but didn't record in time (no skip), until a newer signal of its side for the stock comes: then that one stands for the trade, so it can't be counted twice (Ernest). Manual AI rounds count only held steps (confirmed or waiting). *Bear markets* keeps its own step schedule. Replaces 2026-10-23's "bought at D+1 whatever the user does" and #61's "engine invariance" for Manual strategies; the regulation note's preference for the rules' position is set aside (Ernest, #282).
- 2026-11-24 · STL's own backtest, how it works ([backtest.md](backtest.md)). Every line gets the same money, monthly limit, costs and formulas, and runs the app's engine. Defaults: 16 years in 4-year periods, each a fresh test (*All* is their average); €6,000, €100 per trade, up to 5 a stock, at most €400 a month with unspent limit carried over in full; fee 0.1% + spread 0.1% a trade, no fixed part, no currency fee; dividends in through adjusted prices (tax pending). Stocks: the test draft, 60 S&P 500 stocks by sector and size with a fixed seed, drawn at the first period's start and rolled at each later one, checked for being representative and for 4–5 candidates a year per stock template (never re-drawn to look better); or the strategy's own list or one stock, the latest period by default. Three baselines: 1 *S&P 500, monthly* (the market), 2 *same days, same money, same list* (the choice of stocks), 3 *same stocks, monthly* (the timing, read against 2). Results money-weighted (IRR on the money invested), with the budget at work. Data: a monthly snapshot of the draft's stocks, SPY and the starter lists; all members downloaded only to draw the draft; other stocks and indexes fetched when a test runs. Its own owner-only page, entered from the strategy page, Advanced settings, *Review a template* and the stock page, with a preview panel in Advanced settings (*Rerun tests*, *Default / Saved / Current*). Dropped on the way: random draws, the whole-budget view, *S&P 500, same days*, *Saved tests* in v1. (Ernest, #265)

- 2026-11-22 · *The comeback*'s "Candidate since" is the first day of the stock's current unbroken run as a candidate, as *Winners at a discount*'s already was, instead of the day it was last beaten down: a stock that stays down is beaten down again every day, so the date moved forward daily and an old candidate read as new (CRM, a candidate since 31 Jul, read "since Nov 20"; Ernest's feedback). - 2026-11-22 · A user's pushes go out at least a minute apart (`PUSH_GAP_SECONDS`, 60 by default, 0 in the tests): Ernest saw only one of several pushes sent within the same second, though both push services accepted each one and each had its own tag. The send waits for the gap, one push at a time in the API (a lock, so the check's pushes and the hourly run's can't meet), and the check now pushes after its emails, so the waits don't hold them. A check with several pushes for one user still holds the price worker a minute per extra push, and with it a strategy's check at once and the admin's Run now; fine at this app's size. If it grows: a push queue like the email one. (Ernest, #273)
- 2026-11-22 · *The comeback*'s first step needs a close above the last close, in place of a close above the one 5 trading days before (#257), and a price that comes back into the buy area from above needs no volume, only that up day: a pullback after the turn ran is a better price, and trading is usually light on one (Ernest). "From above": the last close outside the window, since the recent low, was above the buy area; one that dipped below the buy line comes from below again and needs the volume. A stock with no volume data gets no pullback signal, as it gets no volume one. No date and no migration, as in #264: the replay follows the recorded signals on checked days (#261), so past buys can't move. One gap, accepted as for #264: a checked day the budget held back ("No budget left") re-runs today's rule, so the rules' own steps on such a day can change, though no trade or signal does. A stored check from before the deploy reads its 5-day change as "on the day" until the next daily check rewrites it. That also retires #257's dated param, `VolumeOnRiseFrom` (a key still in saved params is ignored). The golden tests keep the POC's first step through the engine option `pocTurn` (was `pocChaseLimit`). A signal is bought the next day in the rules' replay, so a down day can't end an open one. On the six golden runs, with #268's paced later steps, only MOD changed: its first buy moved from 5 Oct 2021 (a down day, 12.20) to a pullback on 14 Feb 2022 (10.32), and its five steps average 11.10 instead of 11.97 (the old first three were 24–34% down 120 days later, the new five all up); the other five are unchanged. Too small a sample to call it better; the template's tested results stay the POC's. (#271)
- 2026-11-21 · *The comeback*'s later steps come at a pace: one every 10 trading days after the last buy (`stepEveryDays`, an Advanced setting, 5–30), while the close is above the last step's price (the open it was bought at), instead of the POC's four milestones (back above the SMA 200, golden cross, near the old high, a breakout). Ernest wants the templates simpler, and the milestones were all signs the price went higher. For every strategy, with no date or migration (#261): a held position switches on the first check after the deploy, its pace counting from its last buy, and one that had used its milestones with steps left can get steps again. On days checked before the deploy whose budget was full, the replay counts the pace's steps as held by the budget (the rules carry on as if bought, as on any full-budget day), so a comeback stock held through a full budget can read its steps used. Accepted. A step's Manual signal stays open while the close is above the step price it came from (for the turn, above its own buy price; no milestone signal was waiting in production at the deploy), with no upper limit, as *Bear markets*' does. The milestone code, settings and texts are removed (Ernest: "don't leave disabled code, it's always recoverable from git history"); the golden tests compare the comeback with the POC up to its first step held. The results are still the milestone rule's, and How we tested says so until STL's own backtest (Milestone 4). (Ernest, #268)
- 2026-11-21 · *Winners at a discount* stacks two averages, SMA 50 > SMA 200 (with the SMA 200 rising), instead of the POC's three (SMA 50 > SMA 150 > SMA 200), for every strategy and with no setting: Ernest wants the templates simpler, and a replay on 22 stocks (the 9 starter stocks and 13 others) since 2000 gave about 11% more signals with the same results after them (`docs/dip-trend-rule-study.md`). Like #264, no date and no migration: since #261 the replay follows the recorded signals on checked days, so a past buy can't move. One rare edge, as in #264: a checked day whose budget was full and that the looser rule now puts in the buy window replays as a budget-held step, which can shift that stock's step count. And on the first check after the deploy, a stock whose SMA 50 sits between the SMA 200 and the SMA 150 becomes a candidate with no price move, and one already in the zone gets a buy signal (an alert in Manual), as any looser rule would. Accepted. The POC's stack stays as an engine option for the golden tests (`pocStack`). The template's results are still the three-average rule's, and How we tested says so until STL's own backtest (Milestone 4) replaces them. Replaces #260's two-or-three setting. (Ernest, #267)
- 2026-11-17 · *The comeback*'s first step counts its chase limit from the recent low, like its buy line: the turn buys from +10% to +20% above the low (buy line 10%, chase 10%), not up to 10% above the line (+21%, the POC's rule); at the defaults (10%, 8%) +10% to +18% instead of +18.8%. - 2026-11-12 · During a US session the check at once runs too: before a stock's rules run it fetches that stock's latest price (not stored), and a signal it finds trades at that price, dated that day; a stock without one from after the last finished session (the fetch failed, nothing traded yet today, a holiday) is left to the daily run. - 2026-11-12 · A strategy is checked at once when it starts or its list gains a stock, not at the next daily check: the price worker fetches what its stocks lack and runs its rules up to the last finished session, one at a time with the daily run. Its first check is the latest finished session at that moment, as for the daily run, and a signal there trades at the next open. While a US session is in progress that open is already known, so the check waits for the daily run after the close: a simulation never buys at a price the user could see (review of #219; Ernest asked which price fills). A signal's page fetches the next open when it isn't stored, and prefills the close until it is. (Ernest's feedback; assumptions in the PR)
